Human Check / Vulcet experiment

Human verification interrupts the thing you came here to do.

Image grids. Repeated challenges. A separate task just to continue.

What if verification became part of the interaction instead?

This interaction observes the shape, timing and variation of your movement — locally in your browser.

What just happened?

You weren’t solving a puzzle. The destination was obvious. The experiment watched how the circle reached the target — trajectory geometry, timing, velocity structure, acceleration, jerk, curvature, and small corrections during target acquisition.

All of that was computed locally in your browser. No movement data was sent anywhere.

Read the research note →

The point isn’t the circle.

A real verification system could never responsibly decide “human or bot” from one drag. Production verification needs multiple signals, server-side security, anti-replay, privacy care and accessible alternatives.

This prototype isolates one research question: how much evidence of human motor behaviour exists in a single intentional movement — and could that evidence emerge from interaction instead of interruption?

Keyboard completion uses an accessible alternative path. It is not scored as if it were mouse biometrics. Unusual motor patterns are similarity to a provisional calibration — not proof of personhood.

Verification should happen around the user — not against them.

This experiment does not propose a finished security system. It asks whether verification can become less disruptive when interaction itself becomes part of the signal.
